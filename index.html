Why this series exists

Across workforce projects with the City of Rochester, RochesterWorks, and employers, TogetherNow kept hearing about the same barriers. They did not come from a lack of effort. They came from weak coordination across the systems people rely on to find work and keep it.

At the final RochesterWorks prototyping session, partners said many of these challenges crossed organizational boundaries and would take a broader community conversation to address. The Workforce Ecosystem Design Series came out of that feedback. It brings together workforce, education, human services, employers, and community supports, each of which has been working on these problems from where it sits.

How we approached the work

TogetherNow uses human-centered design for challenges no single sector can solve alone. The process began by listening to community members, providers, and employers through interviews, surveys, and field research, then mapping their experiences across the workforce journey into a shared story partners could work from. Later stages identify opportunities for change, develop prototypes with partners, and test them as pilots that can be adjusted and, where they work, expanded.

The path to work has three phases

When the experiences of community members, providers, and employers were mapped side by side, the gaps in the workforce journey fell into three phases. Each session in the series has built on that framework.

Find
  • Learning what opportunities exist
  • Navigating applications and eligibility
  • Accessing digital tools
  • Building readiness
Get
  • Interviews and onboarding
  • Paperwork and benefits
  • Lining up transportation, childcare, and schedules
Keep
  • Stability through life disruptions
  • Balancing work and family
  • Advancement
  • Knowing where to turn when something changes

What happened in each session

Session 1, November 19, 2025

Naming the barriers

The first session set out to build a shared understanding of the barriers rising to the surface and how the work might take shape. Partners described who their organizations serve, then mapped barriers at each stage of Find, Get, Keep. They also reviewed a proposed workflow in which a navigator addresses social care needs and an advisor supports career and training goals, with both staying in contact with employers and providers.

Session 2, March 18, 2026

Finding what already works

Session two opened with Tanya's journey and a discussion of how her story could have gone differently with the right supports in place. Partners then looked at how their own organizations address transportation, childcare, workforce readiness, and system coordination today, including informal workarounds. Groups identified which existing efforts qualify as system solutions, meaning they can scale, are not limited to one zip code, and do not have overly restrictive eligibility. The session closed with partners ranking the remaining gaps in the order the group should take them on.

Session 3, June 23, 2026

Designing a connected path

Session three looked at two changes affecting workforce in the region: new investment in semiconductor manufacturing, and federal changes that expand work requirements for SNAP and Medicaid. Partners heard from the Greater Rochester Chamber about Step Up, which gives employers a single front door to semiconductor talent, and from CAO of WNY about the HOPE Initiative, a pilot supporting families with financial literacy, workforce readiness, and employment. Working in groups, partners then designed how existing organizations could support one person from getting connected through training, employment, and the new barriers that come up after. They also worked out what partner structure would keep the effort organized and accountable.

Session 4, October 20, 2026

Imagining 2028

The final session asked what this work should look like two years from now. Partners answered by writing the headlines they hope to read in 2028, collected in Headlines from 2028.

What we prioritized together

What people need

  • Reliable transportation and childcare
  • Accessible pathways to readiness, training, and employment
  • Support navigating the transition into work
  • Ongoing support to stay employed and grow

What the ecosystem needs

  • Visibility into resources and partner capacity
  • Support that connects workforce and social care
  • Clearer pathways between programs, services, and employers
  • Shared approaches to intake, screening, and referral
