Why this series exists

Through workforce projects with the City of Rochester, RochesterWorks, and local employers, TogetherNow kept hearing about the same barriers. People were working hard to find and keep jobs, and what held them back was how loosely the systems around them were connected.

Partners in that work said many of these challenges crossed organizational lines and needed a broader community conversation. The Workforce Ecosystem Design Series grew out of that, bringing workforce, education, human services, employers, and community partners together to work on the problem as a group.

The path to work has three phases

Community members, providers, and employers described the journey the same way, and every session since has built on it.

Find
  • Learning what opportunities exist
  • Navigating applications and eligibility
  • Accessing digital tools
  • Building readiness
Get
  • Interviews and onboarding
  • Paperwork and benefits
  • Lining up transportation, childcare, and schedules
Keep
  • Stability through life disruptions
  • Balancing work and family
  • Advancement
  • Knowing where to turn when something changes

What happened in each session

Session 1, November 19, 2025

Naming the barriers

The series began by building a shared picture of the problem. Partners from across sectors described who they serve and where barriers show up, then mapped those barriers against each stage of Find, Get, Keep. The group also looked at a way of working in which a navigator helps with social care needs and an advisor helps with career and training goals, so a person has support on both fronts at once.

Session 2, March 18, 2026

Finding what already works

Session two followed one person's story, Tanya's, to see where a path to work can come apart and what might have kept it together. Partners then looked at how their own organizations address transportation, childcare, workforce readiness, and system coordination today. Together they identified which efforts could work as system solutions, ones that can grow and reach people beyond a single neighborhood or program, and ranked the remaining gaps in the order the group felt they should be addressed.

Session 3, June 23, 2026

Designing a connected path

Session three turned to what is changing in the region. Partners heard about Step Up, the Greater Rochester Chamber's effort to prepare local talent for new semiconductor jobs, and the HOPE Initiative from CAO of WNY, which supports families moving toward employment and financial stability. Working in groups, they designed how existing organizations could support one person from first contact through training, employment, and the challenges that come after. They also considered how partners would stay coordinated and how they would know whether it was working.

Session 4, October 20, 2026

Testing it together

The final session brings this work forward into two proposed pilots and a conversation about how partners can take part.

What we prioritized and where it led

What people need

  • Reliable transportation and childcare
  • Accessible pathways to readiness, training, and employment
  • Support navigating the transition into work
  • Ongoing support to stay employed and grow

What the ecosystem needs

  • Visibility into resources and partner capacity
  • Support that connects workforce and social care
  • Clearer pathways between programs, services, and employers
  • Shared approaches to intake, screening, and referral

On Tuesday we will discuss two proposed pilots that build on this work.

Workforce Ecosystem Access, Entry & Navigation

Tests how RochesterWorks' existing entry and navigation infrastructure can connect more effectively with training, employment, and social care supports, with other organizations taking part through their own entry points.

Partners Ending Homelessness Workforce Partnership

Tests a defined referral pathway between participating housing providers and RochesterWorks navigation, with housing partners continuing to support participants' broader needs.
